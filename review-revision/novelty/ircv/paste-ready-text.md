## Paragraph 1 — motivation and method

> Formal analyses and implementations are usually evaluated separately: a symbolic model proves lemmas over an idealized event vocabulary, while a prototype implementation is tested with concrete traces. We implement a lightweight **implementation-refined compositional verification (IRCV)** bridge that projects both onto one normalized trace schema. On the formal side, we parse the committed Tamarin theory and its proof artifacts to extract the observable action vocabulary and the structural conditions implied by its lemmas; we do not re-run the prover and claim no new proof result. On the implementation side, we run existing scenario fixtures through the TypeScript verifier and the local payment facilitator, then normalize their observable events (principal anchoring, key authorization, commitment and nonce, predicate decision, settlement, consumption, and verification verdict) into the same schema. A differential engine evaluates the model-derived conditions against the normalized implementation traces and reports concrete expected-versus-actual differences for each violation.

## Paragraph 2 — what was measured

> In our evaluation the committed theory contributed seven rules, eleven action labels, and eight lemmas whose verified statuses were copied from the committed result file. Six implementation scenarios were normalized: an honest exact-settlement control, an honest delegated settlement, two delegated predicate violations, a nonce replay, and a revocation-aware stale-epoch case. Four scenarios were comparable to the formal alphabet and two were explicitly marked out-of-model. The differential engine matched all six recorded expectations; the two predicate violations were flagged by the predicate-admission condition, while the replay was prevented by the facilitator before a second nonce consumption. A separate serialization check compared commitment digest functions against an independent encoding mirror (17 of 17 matches) and reported, without modifying, a defect in the existing multi-payee address-normalization path. A seeded cross-language test executed the real Solidity guard over 64 generated cases (512 field comparisons, all matched), and a generated adversarial script reproduced the stale-epoch counterexample: the local facilitator accepted the stale payment nonce while the opt-in verifier gate rejected it with an identity-epoch-stale violation.

## Paragraph 3 — scope statement (recommended to keep)

> These experiments establish a bounded correspondence, not a refinement theorem. The formal theory models the delegated (predicate) flow only, so exact-settlement traces and revocation epochs lie outside its alphabet and are reported as such. The normalization is an abstract event projection: symbolic term equality is approximated by concrete digests and addresses, and only six scenarios were examined. We therefore report the observed matches and the uncovered cases explicitly, and we do not claim that the implementation refines the model beyond the traces tested.

## Short version (one paragraph)

> We bridge a committed Tamarin model and a TypeScript implementation through a shared trace schema, extract six structural conditions from the model's lemmas, and check them against normalized traces from six existing scenarios. Four scenarios are comparable and all recorded expectations are matched; two are reported out-of-model. Commitment serialization matches an independent mirror in 17 of 17 checks, a Solidity-executed property test matches 512 of 512 fields across 64 seeded cases, and a generated adversarial script reproduces a stale-epoch rejection. No new proof is claimed, and the uncovered modes are stated explicitly.
